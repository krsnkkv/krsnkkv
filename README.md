### 👂 Can you hear the rāga?

<details>
<summary>1. The line at the top of this page only ever touches five notes: S R₂ G₃ P D₂. Which rāga is it tracing?</summary>
<br>

**Mohanam.** The same five notes are Bhoopali in Hindustani music, and the major pentatonic scale almost everywhere else.
</details>

<details>
<summary>2. S R₁ G₃ M₁ P D₁ N₃ Ṡ. Most Carnatic students sing this scale before any other. What is it called?</summary>
<br>

**Mayamalavagowla**, the 15th melakarta. The very first exercises are taught in it.
</details>

<details>
<summary>3. Can two different rāgas be built from the same notes?</summary>
<br>

**Yes.** Bhoopali and Deshkar share the same five notes, and allied Carnatic pairs like Darbar and Nayaki sit close enough to trip up listeners. What separates them is where the melody dwells, how the notes are ornamented, and which phrases keep returning.

That is the idea behind my dissertation. A model built on 16 features describing where a melody dwells reached 67.8% weighted F1 and beat a BiLSTM baseline by 31 points, and it confused rāgas within the same family about five times as often as rāgas from different families.
</details>
